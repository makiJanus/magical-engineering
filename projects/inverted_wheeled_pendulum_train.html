This robot can navigate while keeping its balance

The Problem

Mohammad Shoaib Babar is a master's candidate studying how a two-wheeled inverted-pendulum robot can balance and perform basic movements in Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab. I helped build the simulation, training, and testing pipeline for that research. This article presents a related experiment using my own two-wheeled robot; it is a spin-off of the collaboration rather than a complete account of the original research project.

Using Plain Deep Reinforcement Learning

The robot must learn to balance, orient toward a goal, and move without falling. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) addresses this by repeatedly applying actions, observing the resulting state, and updating a policy according to a reward function.

In this experiment, the reward function provides feedback for useful behavior such as remaining upright, moving toward a target, and avoiding failure. The quality of that reward design strongly affects what the policy learns.

Initially, I attempted to train balance, navigation, and goal stopping in a single stage. The combined task was too difficult to learn reliably with the chosen training setup, so the policy did not acquire a stable sequence of behaviors.

Curriculum Learning

The solution was to divide the problem into simpler tasks and train the robot step by step—a technique called curriculum learning. The idea is to use the results of the first stage of training (e.g., balancing) to help with the next stage (e.g., navigating).

The robot’s sensors play a key role in this process. They provide feedback about the consequences of its actions, allowing us to guide its learning more effectively.

Each stage builds on the previous one. The policy first preserves the skills already learned, then adapts them to the next objective instead of discovering every behavior from scratch.

Navigation

With the robot trained to move, the next challenge was navigating in realistic environments. For this, I created a virtual house using Unreal Engine 5, which integrates smoothly with IsaacSim.

IsaacSim provides tools for generating occupancy maps of environments. These maps are then used with the A* algorithm to generate paths.

The paths from A* are smoothed with Bezier curves. The resulting trajectory is then sampled and damped into a sequence of goal points that the robot can follow, reducing abrupt changes in direction.

Results

In simulation, the robot can move between selected points without falling and follow predefined paths through a house while navigating around furniture and tight corners. These results demonstrate the behavior in Isaac Sim; they do not yet establish performance on the physical robot.

This is the first phase of a larger project. The next stage is a sim-to-real transfer, which will require validating the dynamics model, sensors, latency, and control behavior on the physical robot.
